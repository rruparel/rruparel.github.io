Read Your Way

Nobody in the study read this diabetes handout the same way. Answer four quick questions, and the page will match you with the participant you read most like, then adjust the page with the supports that helped them.

What the study foundPeople often read health pages on behalf of someone else.
 They decide which voices to trust: doctors, daughters, pharmacists, AI.
 One short line can change who they think is to blame.

Your answers changed this page. In the study, the page also changed its readers.

After reading that stress or illness can raise blood sugar, P4 rethought what “poorly controlled” means:

“Now I think, maybe sometimes you did your part and still it went up.”

Each support here started as something a participant needed. Tap any tag on the page to read the moment behind it. To see how each person read the original page, open Read Five Ways.

A class prototype, not medical advice. Handout content is condensed from pages 9 and 10 of an American Diabetes Association guide for people living with type 2 diabetes. Each added support is tagged with the participant whose reading it came from. Five think-aloud interviews, September 2026. Roma Ruparel, INFO 272.
